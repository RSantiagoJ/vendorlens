HIGHER EDUCATION VENDOR EVALUATION POLICY — DISTILLED RULES FOR VENDORLENS RISK AGENT
Source: Payroll Processing Services Procurement

SECTION 1: SECURITY VENDOR MANAGEMENT

All payroll processing vendors must complete a security risk questionnaire before
contract execution. Payroll data is classified as highly sensitive; the review confirms:

- No duplication of existing institutional payroll or HR tool functionality
- Fits within the current technology ecosystem and integrates with existing HRIS/ERP
- Supports centralized payroll administration across all campuses and units
- Allows the institution to scale headcount without excessive cost increases

All of the following teams must provide final approval before contract execution:

- Legal Team: contract documentation, applicable data privacy compliance
- Finance Team: spend against budget and TCO validation
- CHRO / HR Leadership: workforce data governance, employee experience, and compliance
- CISO: payroll data security, encryption standards, and access controls
- Payroll Director: processing accuracy, tax filing, and operational requirements

SECTION 2: SECURITY CERTIFICATION REQUIREMENTS

- SOC 2 Type II (current) OR ISO 27001 certification required for payroll processing systems
- SOC 2 Type I does not satisfy this requirement
- Certifications in progress do not satisfy this requirement
- SOC 1 Type II (SSAE 18) required separately to cover payroll processing controls

SECTION 3: DATA PROCESSING AND GOVERNANCE

- A Data Processing Agreement (DPA) is required before contract execution
- DPA must address: FERPA (student employee records), GLBA, applicable state data
  privacy laws, IRS data handling requirements, and payroll data confidentiality
- Institution retains full ownership of all payroll, tax, and employee data at all times
- Vendor may not use payroll or employee data for any purpose beyond direct service
  delivery without explicit written approval from the CHRO and CIO
- All payroll data must be exportable in standard formats (CSV, XML) at no cost
  within 10 business days of contract termination
- Payroll data must be encrypted in transit (TLS 1.2 minimum) and at rest (AES-256)
- W-2 and 1099 data retained for minimum 7 years per IRS requirements

SECTION 4: CONTRACT TERM REQUIREMENTS

- Termination for convenience: maximum 90 days notice, no financial penalty
- Early termination fees not permitted
- Liability cap minimum: $500,000 OR two times annual contract value, whichever is greater
- IP ownership of custom reports, integrations, and configurations must remain with institution
- Vendors may not modify pricing or terms unilaterally mid-contract
- Governing law: institution's home state
- Auto-renewal opt-out: minimum 60 days notice required
- Payroll processing errors resulting in underpayment or overpayment: vendor bears
  cost of correction, reprocessing, and any associated tax amendments

SECTION 5: SERVICE LEVEL REQUIREMENTS

- Payroll processing SLA: payroll must complete within the agreed processing window
  (typically 2 business days before pay date) with financial penalties for breach
- System availability: 99.9% uptime during business hours; 99.5% overall
- Tax filing: all federal, state, and local tax filings submitted on time; vendor
  bears any IRS or state penalties resulting from late or inaccurate filings
- Year-end processing (W-2, 1099): completed and delivered by January 15 for prior year
- Dedicated support during open enrollment, year-end, and critical payroll windows

SECTION 6: INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY

All custom configurations, reports, workflows, and integrations developed during the
contract and paid for with institutional funds vest with the institution at termination.
Vendor retains rights to base platform IP only.

SECTION 7: REGULATORY COMPLIANCE

- IRS reporting obligations: W-2, 1099-NEC, 1099-MISC, ACA 1095-C supported and filed
- Federal and multi-state payroll tax filing and remittance managed by vendor
- FLSA compliance (overtime, minimum wage) supported
- FMLA and state leave law compliance tracking supported
- Garnishment processing (child support, tax levies, creditor garnishments) supported

SECTION 8: ASSIGNMENT AND DELEGATION

- Contractor may not assign or transfer contract without prior written consent
- Subcontractors processing payroll or tax data require prior written approval
  and must meet the same SOC 2 Type II security requirements

SECTION 9: CHOICE OF LAW

- Governed by laws of the institution's home state
- Without giving effect to conflicts of law principles

SECTION 10: HIGH SEVERITY RISK TRIGGERS

The following conditions require CHRO, CFO, or VP-level approval and are
contract blockers:

- No Data Processing Agreement (DPA) provided or mentioned
- No current SOC 2 Type II or ISO 27001 certification
- No SOC 1 Type II (SSAE 18) for payroll processing controls
- Vendor not liable for IRS/state tax penalties caused by vendor errors
- Payroll error correction costs borne by the institution
- Institution data not explicitly owned by the institution
- Payroll data not exportable in standard formats at no cost upon termination
- IP ownership of configurations or custom work product transferred to vendor
- Vendor right to modify pricing or terms unilaterally mid-contract
- Liability cap below $500,000 or below two times annual contract value
- Auto-renewal opt-out window shorter than 60 days
- Early termination fee or penalty present
- Governing law not institution's home state
- No contractual SLA with penalties for payroll processing failures
- Vendor use of payroll or employee data for product improvement or AI training
